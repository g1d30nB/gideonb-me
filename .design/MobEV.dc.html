The challenge

Users could set a charging schedule. 92 per cent completed the task in testing and rated its ease at 5.15 against a benchmark of 5.5. They got to the end without trusting what they had set, because the screen gave them a control and said nothing about what it would do.

Whose work

The study was run by my research team, Amy Mullin and Tatiana Papaioannou. My part was the function that ran it, the standard it was held to, and the decision taken from what it found. What was found, and what was decided

The finding.

New users needed the outcome stated to trust the schedule they had just set. Advanced users needed it stated to work out why a schedule had misbehaved. One change served both.

The standard.

Three rounds between May and July 2025, 25 participants across the UK, Germany, Norway and Sweden, the same task set each round, with task completion, ease and the System Usability Scale recorded every time.

The decision.

State the outcome on the screen, in the terms of the schedule the user has just set.

What changed

Task completion across the EV domain moved from 78 per cent to 87 per cent over three rounds. The research was the team’s. The standard and the decision were mine.
